# PostgreSQL pool-20 admission diagnostic

Run on 2026-09-27 at committed source `38ebf7095f4bcbc96fe9562bff89f36e0d0c36e4` (`git_dirty=false`). This is **one diagnostic run**, not a three-run comparison or evidence of a throughput regression. The workload submitted 100,000 jobs through 32 concurrent clients, with no agents, at unlimited target rate. The application had 4 CPUs / 6 GiB, and a separate PostgreSQL 15 container had 4 CPUs / 4 GiB. The benchmark database was dropped and recreated before the run; the application pool remained at **20**. No application code or pool settings were changed.

The run submitted and stored all 100,000 jobs with zero submission errors or OOM kills. Admission was **10,843 jobs/s**, with p50 **2.44 ms**, p95 **5.84 ms**, and p99 **12.18 ms**. PostgreSQL consumed **33.29 CPU-seconds** over the **9.22-second** submission window, about **3.61 of its 4 cores**. The application container consumed **23.76 CPU-seconds**, about **2.58 of its 4 cores**. PostgreSQL incurred **0.73 seconds of CPU throttling**. The final Prometheus snapshot recorded **77 scheduler runs** totaling **8.13 seconds of scheduler-call time** and **84.32 seconds of cumulative Go SQL connection wait** across goroutines; neither cumulative measure is elapsed wall time.

Immediately after the run and **before** running `EXPLAIN`, `pg_stat_database` for the fresh benchmark database reported **524,632,064 bytes** of temporary-file writes (about 500 MiB), zero deadlocks, and about 200,375 inserted tuples. This database-level counter does not attribute temporary bytes to a specific query. Brief `pg_stat_activity` samples during admission showed active backends waiting on `IO:WALSync` and `LWLock:WALWrite` as well as executing on CPU; no heavyweight `Lock` wait was observed in those samples. The sampling covered only part of the submission window and does not quantify total time in each wait state.

The current scheduler calls `ListPendingJobs` even when there are no agents. That query selects all eligible pending jobs and orders them by `priority DESC, id`. Against the final 100k-job database, `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` reported a **parallel sequential scan**, three **external-merge sort** workers, **3,372 temporary buffers written**, and **176.47 ms execution time** for this single query. The leader and workers reported approximately 9.7, 8.5, and 8.7 MiB of sort spill respectively. `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` ran **after** the database-level temporary-byte snapshot, so its own I/O did not contribute to the 500-MiB figure above. The repeated full-queue scan/sort is a concrete source of avoidable work in this *no-agent* scenario; these observations do not establish that it is the sole admission bottleneck.

Next controlled change to test: skip loading/sorting pending jobs when there are no healthy nodes, while preserving wakeup when a node registers. For workloads with agents, separately investigate bounded candidate selection. Keep the connection pool at 20 and rerun the same benchmark three times before claiming a throughput improvement. The four raw JSON, Prometheus, application-resource, and PostgreSQL-resource files are labeled `pg20-profdiag-100k` in this directory.
